## P8-O1: arbitrary finite conditional observation designs

Let X be a finite set of ordered physical opponent states. An experiment e has
a finite index set Y_e and a disclosed conditional probability matrix
K_e(y,x)≥0, with each row summing to one. A row specifies the lottery over actual
states in that experimental context. This is a conditional-state design;
a forward signal channel P(y|x) requires a specified prior before conversion
by Bayes' rule, and is not silently the same matrix. For a payoff table f,
the observed commitment values are K_e f. Stack all available rows into K.

**Theorem.** Two tables are observationally equivalent iff their difference
belongs to N=ker K=intersection_e ker K_e. A linear functional l·f is identifiable
iff l belongs to the row span of K. All entries are identified iff rank K=|X|.
These statements remain true with restricted disclosed contexts: simply remove
the unavailable rows. Adding observations can only decrease N.

Proof. The first assertion is subtraction. If l is in the row span its value
is the corresponding combination of observations. Conversely a functional
vanishing on ker K is in its orthogonal complement, which equals the row span
by finite-dimensional rank-nullity. Otherwise two tables separated by l have
identical observations. Taking all coordinate functionals gives full recovery.
This is standard linear inverse-problem algebra, not a novelty claim. ∎

This formulation handles unequal opponent action sets: X=product_j A_j and K
must actually be defined on this product. There is no coordinate-swap operator
between unequal A_j without additional physical encodings. A channel may use
explicit action conversions, including stochastic ones; then its disclosed
matrix, not an assumed symmetric-group representation, governs identification.
Even equal cardinalities require disclosed bijections to interpret a swap.

## P8-O2: uniform subgroup masks and the limits of a generated group

If a finite group acts on X and an experiment uniformly conceals H≤G, write
P_H=|H|^{-1}sum_{h in H}rho(h), on R^X. With the standard inner product P_H is
the orthogonal projection onto H-invariant tables. Thus

    N = intersection_e (Fix H_e)^perp
      = (sum_e Fix H_e)^perp.

Indeed P_H is self-adjoint by inversion, idempotent by counting products, and
its image consists exactly of invariant tables. This gives the stated kernels.
The sum of fixed subspaces is essential: in general it cannot be replaced by
the fixed space of the group generated by the H_e. Pair masks are special:
ker((I+T)/2) is the −1 eigenspace of that transposition, which supplies the
alternating character argument of P7-O1.

For example, binary tables on three positions have dimension eight. Concealing
all three positions uniformly leaves only four histogram averages, so the
kernel has dimension four. Two pair masks, on {1,2} and {2,3}, have joint kernel
zero. Both collections generate S3. The generated group alone therefore does
not determine the information loss. Nor can one replace uniform subgroup
masking by arbitrary probabilities without changing the answer.

## P8-O3: the pair threshold fails under arbitrarily small known bias

Let T be one coordinate transposition and B_q=qI+(1−q)T, 0≤q≤1. Then for
q≠1/2,

    B_q^{-1} = [qI−(1−q)T]/(2q−1).

Proof: multiply and use T²=I. Equivalently B_q has eigenvalue one on symmetric
tables and 2q−1 on antisymmetric tables. Thus ONE known biased pair experiment,
when all reference-state contexts are available, recovers every table, whatever
m,k. At q=1/2 it leaves exactly the usual alternating-in-that-pair kernel.

For k≥2 and m≥2 both eigenspaces occur; the Euclidean minimum singular value
is |2q−1| and inversion amplifies worst-case Euclidean observation noise by
1/|2q−1|. Thus exact identifiability is discontinuous at perfect concealment,
whereas stable recovery becomes poor continuously near it. When there is only
one action T=I and this conditioning qualification does not apply. The inverse
has signed coefficients in general: this is a reconstruction of known expected
values, not a stochastic garbling or a Blackwell equivalence claim.

## P8-O4: a sharp larger-subset threshold

Let X=A^m with |A|=k≥1. For every r-subset B of the positions, 2≤r≤m, observe
the uniform S_B average P_B f, at every reference profile. Let S^lambda be the
Specht module and M_lambda the Schur multiplicity space in the characteristic-
zero tensor-power decomposition

    (C^k)^{tensor m} = direct_sum_{lambda partitions m, length(lambda)≤k}
                         M_lambda tensor S^lambda.

**Theorem (standard representation-theoretic application).** The complexified
joint invisible subspace is precisely the sum of the full isotypic components
indexed by lambda_1<r. Its dimension is

    sum_{lambda partitions m, length(lambda)≤k, lambda_1<r}
                         dim(M_lambda) dim(S^lambda).

Consequently all ordered entries are recovered iff

    m > k(r−1).

The real kernel has the same dimension and the same zero/nonzero criterion.
This requires the complete collection of r-subsets. Incomplete collections
remain governed by P8-O1/O2; no analogous numeric threshold is asserted.

Proof. Start with H=S_{1,...,r}. Every other subgroup S_B is conjugate to H,
and conversely these are all its conjugates in S_m. On an irreducible S^lambda,
P_H projects onto its H-invariants. The standard symmetric-group restriction
branching rule says that restriction to S_r contains the trivial representation
S^(r) exactly when the diagram lambda contains the row (r), equivalently
lambda_1≥r. To see the equivalence explicitly, iterated restriction removes
one corner at a time. A path ending at (r) exists exactly when (r) is a
subdiagram: every skew diagram lambda/(r) has a corner-removal ordering.
Multiplicity is the number of these paths, so positivity is all that matters.

If lambda_1<r then P_H is zero and all conjugate averages are zero. If
lambda_1≥r, the span of all conjugate images rho(g)Fix(H) is a nonzero invariant
subspace of S^lambda, hence the whole irreducible module. The intersection of
the conjugate kernels is its orthogonal complement, hence zero. More directly,
this intersection is invariant under S_m and cannot be a proper nonzero
submodule. Each average acts as identity on M_lambda and its Specht action on
the second factor, so multiplicity causes neither extra kernels nor accidental
cancellation between isotypic components. The central isotypic projections
commute with every P_B; kernels therefore split by those components. This
proves the displayed decomposition and dimension.

The tensor-power decomposition has nonzero M_lambda exactly when the diagram
has at most k rows. A nonempty partition of m with at most k rows, each of
length at most r−1, exists iff m≤k(r−1). Necessity is box counting. For
sufficiency fill rows of length r−1, then one shorter final row; the number
of rows is ceil(m/(r−1))≤k. Finally real matrices have the same rank over R
and C. ∎

For r=2 only the one-column partition survives, giving wedge^m(C^k) and
binomial(k,m), exactly the connected all-pairs case P7-O1. For r=3,k=2,
all triple masks leave a two-dimensional kernel at m=4 (shape (2,2)) but
recover all 32 entries at m=5. For m=3,k=2 a single triple mask has a
four-dimensional kernel. Larger hidden subsets can therefore require more
opponents, not fewer, to recover ordered entries. The count of contexts here
is binomial(m,r); no minimal-budget claim is made for r>2.

Source and review qualification. Schur–Weyl decomposition and symmetric-group
branching are established mathematics. Directly inspected primary author-hosted
exposition: Barcelo–Ram, *Combinatorial Representation Theory*, Appendix A5,
Theorem A5.1/Corollary A5.2,
https://math.soimeme.org/~arunram/Preprints/CRTAppendixASectionA5.html
(the author attributes the duality to Schur and Weyl). Also inspected the
Gruson–Serganova book manuscript contents and symmetric-group discussion:
https://math.berkeley.edu/~serganov/math252/Bookrep.pdf . For the branching dependency, directly inspected Ellers–Murray,
*Branching Rules for Specht Modules* (2004), introduction p.1 and restriction
filtration p.3, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0408088 . Its introduction explicitly
states characteristic-zero restriction as the direct sum over corner removals;
the paper credits the classical rule and gives its own modular refinements.
This closes the statement-source gap, not an independent reconstruction of
that classical theorem. Neither the application nor its proof is claimed as
new group theory.
The argument above makes the precise dependency inspectable. Independent
representation-theorist scrutiny remains required before publication.

## P8-O5: payoff means and robust consequence distributions are different

Let f,h:X→C be deterministic consequence tables on one common finite ordered
physical consequence set. Each row of K induces a consequence lottery. Equality
of expectations for every common strictly increasing VNM utility is equivalent
to equality of those lotteries (the finite upper-tail proof is P7-O4).
Consequently robust equality is exactly

    K(1_{f=c}−1_{h=c})=0 for every c in C.

This applies P8-O1 separately to indicators, rather than to a single arbitrary
numeric encoding. Full column rank is sufficient but is NOT necessary for
recovering deterministic consequence tables from robust tests.

**Theorem.** For |C|≥2, the design universally identifies every deterministic
C-valued table iff

    ker K intersection {−1,0,1}^X = {0}.

Proof. If two different tables have the same observed laws, at least one
consequence indicator differs; its nonzero difference is a ternary vector
in ker K. Conversely take such a vector v. Set binary f=1 on {v=1}, zero
elsewhere, and h=1 on {v=−1}, zero elsewhere. Their indicators differ by v;
the zero-consequence indicators differ by −v. They have identical observed
laws and differ pointwise. Any common ordered C with two selected values
embeds this witness. ∎

Example: one lottery row K=(1/3,2/3) on two states has rank one, with real
kernel spanned by (2,−1). It cannot identify an arbitrary real-valued payoff
mean table. Nevertheless its kernel has no nonzero ternary vector, so the
full consequence law identifies BOTH deterministic consequences: the distinct
atom weights disclose which state supplied each value. More generally distinct
subset sums of known weights give deterministic recovery from a single row.
This is exact recovery; no noise stability claim is made. If consequences are
already stochastic conditional on states, the differences of conditional law
vectors need not be ternary, and this stronger deterministic-table result does
not extend unchanged. One then uses the linear kernel criterion on probability
vectors with their normalization/positivity constraints.

Thus the pair threshold is model-specific twice over: it depends on the
concealment weights and on whether observations reveal one calibrated mean or
a whole consequence law. Neither statistical identification implies a normative
fairness criterion or gives an automatic welfare benefit from disclosure.

## Evidence and proposed manuscript use

Five exact rational tests construct actual observation matrices independently
of the representation formula, check both sides of the triple-mask threshold,
compare biased and uniform masks, test incomplete contexts, and exhaust
three-valued deterministic tables for the distribution/mean separation.
They corroborate finite instances, not the general proof. The strongest
manuscript additions would be the finite-channel criterion, the biased-mask
qualification, and the deterministic-consequence distinction. Keep the all-r
representation theorem as a separately auditable extension pending scrutiny.
