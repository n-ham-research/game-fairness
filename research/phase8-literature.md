## Decision experiments

[Börgers, *Notes on Blackwell Dominance*, 3 December 2023](https://websites.umich.edu/~tborgers/BlackwellDominance.pdf),
author-hosted proof exposition, pp.1–10 directly inspected. Proposition 1
relates informativeness to a stochastic garbling; Proposition 2 relates it to
convex posterior comparisons and optimized decision values. States are fixed
and experiments are varied across all decision problems. Our opportunity
criterion instead fixes role menus and varies common priors. The same
max-linear value functions and convex geometry underlie both; this is strong
reason to describe the envelope theorem as established decision theory
applied here, not a new general characterization. A signed inverse of a
biased mask is not a stochastic garbling. Our channels specify state
lotteries conditional on disclosed contexts, whereas a statistical experiment
normally specifies signals conditional on states; Bayes conversion needs a
prior. Identification of table parameters from repeated exact expectations
is different from improving a participant's one-trial information.
This inspected exposition closes a mathematical-comparison gap, not the
original-printing access gap.

## Preferences over menus

[Dekel–Lipman–Rustichini 2001 author-hosted published paper](https://sites.bu.edu/blipman/files/2021/10/DLR2001.pdf),
printed pp.913–916, Section 4 proof sketch and Lemma 3 directly inspected.
It explicitly represents convex menus by support functions of maximum
expected utility and gives the inverse correspondence. Thus menu/support
geometry is emphatically prior. Its subjective future-taste identification
problem differs from our objective opponent-state role comparisons; that
difference alone does not establish novelty. The comparison uses the support
function passage, not its additive representation theorem. The author's
publication list flags a 2007 corrigendum with Sarver and supplemental proof;
the corrigendum's pp.591–594 were then directly inspected. It strengthens
independence for ordinal EU and continuity for additive EU, and corrects a
support-function norm statement. We invoke none of those stronger results.
The Phase 7 abstract-only source limitation is superseded only for the
specific proof-sketch pages now inspected, not the whole article.

## Historical and normative boundaries

VNM remains cited through the actually verified third edition (1953,
1955 printing), Section 28; the 1944 first edition has not been inspected.
No fresh first-edition access was found or invented. P8-V1 compares coalition
opportunity sets directly in normal form and proves a strict refinement of
security-value equality. It is our model comparison, not VNM's own definition.
Nash's feasible bargaining input, Shapley's coalition input, and participant
assignment are distinct objects; equivariance transfers input stabilizers to
solution symmetries but supplies neither stability nor interpersonal welfare.

## Assessment and next comparisons

The defensible contribution currently is a carefully delimited synthesis:
observable opportunity comparisons, observation kernels, utility calibration,
and shared intervention wiring. The all-r threshold and deterministic-law
criterion are useful candidate applications, not cleared discoveries.
Prioritize (1) expert scrutiny of the information theorems, (2) weighted
mixture identification and invariant-observation literature, (3) empirical
justification of commitment/response protocols. Do not inflate contribution
by relabeling standard support functions as a universal fairness theorem.
