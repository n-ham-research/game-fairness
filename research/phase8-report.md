## Main conclusions

1. **Response-adjusted opportunities (P8-B1–B2).** Action dependence itself
does not destroy the support-function criterion. A fixed disclosed response
policy supplies a new row set, possibly compact and nonpolyhedral. Reactions
to an announced mixed strategy differ from reactions to realized actions.
Role-dependent beliefs change the identified rows by a stochastic pullback.
Belief-dependent feasibility/response requires a new model, not automatic
reuse of the envelope theorem.
2. **Simultaneous play (P8-B3).** Exact/cardinal symmetry transports Nash and
correlated-equilibrium correspondences. It does not equalize an arbitrary
selected equilibrium. A two-player two-action exact-symmetric game has strict
Nash receipts (2,1) and (1,2); invariant selection equalizes expectations.
3. **General observation (P8-O1–O4).** Known channels identify mean tables
modulo their stacked kernel. Uniform subgroup masks lose the orthogonal
complement of the sum of subgroup-fixed spaces, not the fixed space of the
generated group. A known biased pair mask is invertible, with instability
near uniformity. All uniform r-subset masks recover ordered m-opponent,
k-action tables exactly iff m>k(r−1). The last result is a standard
Schur–Weyl/branching application retained outside the paper for scrutiny.
4. **Consequences versus means (P8-O5).** Full increasing-utility tests on
common consequences identify deterministic tables iff the channel kernel
contains no nonzero ternary vector. Full real rank is sufficient but not
necessary. One row of weights (1/3,2/3) separates the two identification
problems. Unequal action alphabets require actual typed channels; no
undefined coordinate permutation is used.
5. **Recovery and calibration (P8-I1/U1).** Shared logged controller wiring
plus informative conditional receipt records identifies simultaneous exact
maps. Within-recipient all-lottery comparisons identify cardinal maps;
pure comparisons identify ordinal maps, under that same measured protocol.
Independent focal comparisons alone miss compatibility of intervention
coordinates. Normalized tables provide representation-invariant algebraic
tests, not a participant's cross-role welfare calibration.
6. **Coherence (P8-C1).** Once an observer is specified, observable path
independence is exactly splitting of the implementation extension modulo
the normal invisible kernel. Payoff-only observations and fully resolved
physical labels give different answers. No intrinsic welfare advantage of
coherence is inferred.
7. **Coalitions (P8-V1).** Normal-form coalition opportunity envelopes refine
security-value equality strictly. This supplies a direct strategic comparison
without attributing it to VNM. Stability and realized equality remain separate.

## Most promising next questions

First, characterize the smallest physically justified intervention design
that separates a particular game's attainable discrepancy set, rather than
all arbitrary real arrays. Second, develop stability bounds and finite-sample
tests: biased masks illustrate why exact invertibility alone is insufficient.
Third, justify commitment and response protocols from a strategic model with
a specified equilibrium selection; do not infer fairness from a correspondence.
Fourth, compare the deterministic-law criterion with weighted-mixture
identification before claiming a new theorem. External review of the all-r
application and observer-quotient criterion should precede broad promotion.
