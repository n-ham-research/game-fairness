# Phase 10 — foundational fairness: state of the research and relationship map

18 October 2026. Author: Nicholas Ham. This corrects the programme's direction;
Agent Games Phase19 is closed and supplies NO new foundational fairness theorem.
Canonical fairness baseline is f75ec9f. No observation-mask, tensor-reconstruction
or symmetry-identification algorithm is extended in this phase.

## What was already established

The migrated utility work U1–U5 (with Ham2011 priority preserved) distinguishes
exact numerical invariance, positive-affine expected-preference invariance and
ordinal pure-outcome invariance. Cardinal and ordinal normalization represent
these groups algebraically; they do not create a participant's cross-role
calibration. Ordinal transformations need not preserve mixed equilibria.

F1–F3 and A1–A5 distinguish focal opportunity transport, opponent histogram
factorization, assignment preference, procedural randomization and solution
selection. Independent utility offsets make within-role preferences consistent
with arbitrary assignment values. Exact symmetry is sufficient for calibrated
role comparison ONLY with corresponding physical transports and transported
behavior. A fixed game symmetry, a rule's equivariance, and equal realized
outcomes are different mathematical objects.

P7-A1/A2 characterize all-belief optimized indifference by downward opportunity
hulls, recovering focal rows under decision-essentiality but not global player
symmetry. P7-A3/A4 separate ordered, exchangeable and optimized IID tests;
P7-A6 treats common-risk tails. P7 coalition/coherence results refute automatic
core stability or welfare costs from label incoherence. P8 adds fixed responses,
role beliefs, equilibrium-selection distinctions and observable controller data.
Most of P8/P9's new technical work concerns observation/identification rather
than new foundational fairness relations. The direction correction is justified;
those results remain valid, but are not rebranded as fairness discoveries.

## Model axes that must be fixed independently

Specify physical consequences and cross-role calibration; deterministic versus
objective risky outcomes; role-known versus role-hidden commitment; independently
fixed own/opponent correspondences; common, role-dependent or ambiguous beliefs;
and what opponents can observe or react to. Also distinguish one participant's
preferences from interpersonal welfare, and feasible procedures from their
selected outcomes. No definition below makes fairness identical to a group.

Within the signed coordination class, P10-O4 fills an intermediate arrow:
local opportunity correspondences assemble for a proposed graph permutation
precisely when it preserves cycle parity. This is a computable invariant and
spanning-tree certificate, not merely the automorphism definition restated.
Its mathematics is classical switching theory; it is not a classification of
all local-to-global game obstructions. Unsigned rigid graphs show that missing
shared interaction identities are another obstacle before signing compatibility.

## Exact, cardinal and ordinal fairness comparisons

Common physical money plus a fixed U permits literal assignment preference.
One common positive-affine recoding preserves it. Independent role-affine
freedom leaves conditional game preferences but underidentifies assignment
comparisons; rank normalization changes the experiment unless justified by the
participant's actual global preference. All common increasing U tests are a
family of cardinal expected-utility models on common ordered consequences,
not a claim that pure ordinal game preferences alone determine lotteries.
State-dependent utility variation is stronger: P10-R3 gives a strict separation.
The graph family's equal attained payoff ranges make its exact/cardinal/ordinal
groups coincide, an explicitly special property proved there.

## Cross-domain framework: what is shared, what is not

There is a useful STANDARD group-action diagram: problem data map to feasible
consequence laws, then a decision or allocation rule selects values/outcomes.
If these maps are equivariant, input stabilizers preserve output SETS; a
single-valued equivariant selection must be fixed by those stabilizers. An
invariant lottery exists by averaging on finite invariant feasible sets, but
an invariant deterministic selection requires an actual fixed point. These
facts were proved locally before Phase10 and are not a new general theorem.

The mathematical content of a fairness axiom depends on the missing maps.
Nash bargaining adds a feasible utility set, disagreement and other solution
axioms; the fixed-problem symmetry axiom alone does not identify the solution.
Kalai–Smorodinsky shares invariance but chooses a different rule. Shapley
requires transferable coalition values and allocation axioms. Varian's no-envy
uses ONE person's preference to compare different physical bundles; externality
counterfactuals must be specified. Random assignment compares lotteries of
objects under each person's ranking, not arbitrary utility numbers across
people. Social-choice anonymity relabels agents and neutrality relabels
alternatives, two separate actions. The original primary bodies/version limits
are recorded in fairness-literature-solutions.md and
fairness-literature-vnm-strategic.md; they were reviewed as prior records here,
not all newly read in Phase10.

No universal fairness principle follows from this common formal pattern.
For example an equivariant rule can have no-envy or efficiency failures;
identical role opportunities can coexist with graph rigidity; and the same
unordered observation can support opposite assignment rankings. These are
mathematical reasons to distinguish the criteria, not objections about words.

## What changed substantively in Phase10

Four investigations now have proofs and retained counterexamples: strong
local-opportunity/global-symmetry impossibility plus a cycle obstruction;
blind assignment characterization and universal alignment; risk-essential
law identification plus a rank-one preference counterexample; and ambiguity/
response-timing opportunity comparison with a sharp bound and role reversal.
They address fairness and symmetry directly. No claim of a priority-cleared
important original theorem, universal fairness equivalence or new signed-graph
invariant is made. Correctness, originality and significance remain separate.
