\begin{abstract}
We separate preservation of numerical utility, preservation of preferences,
and indifference between player assignments in finite games. Exact, cardinal
and ordinal typed automorphism groups admit canonical descriptions, but
within-role preferences do not determine a participant's preferences across
roles. In an explicitly calibrated assignment experiment, focal payoff
transport is equivalent to equality under all action and ordered-belief tests;
individual exact transports suffice for one-shot indifference, whereas
coherent typed implementation is a separate requirement. Opponent anonymity
is distinguished from averaging under hidden identities. This foundation draft
consolidates previously developed proofs and counterexamples; novelty is not
asserted, and no universal characterization of fairness is claimed.
\end{abstract}
