\section{Introduction}
A participant may regard two roles as equally desirable without the game
having a symmetry, and a symmetric game may yield unequal realized payoffs.
To investigate the relationship, we distinguish the utility representation,
the admissible transport of choices and opponents, and the assignment procedure.
The mathematical objective is to derive implications from those independently
specified data, rather than define fairness to mean symmetry.

Ham's thesis \citep{hamthesis} treats cardinal and ordinal game isomorphisms;
his later paper \citep{ham2021} distinguishes several forms of player symmetry.
Our companion project studies their structural group theory. Here we develop
the decision-theoretic foundations needed to ask what those equivalences mean
for assignment preferences. The present draft contains established foundations
and internally checked elementary comparisons, not a completed new fairness theory.

Historical terminology must retain its domain. In the inspected third edition,
von Neumann and Morgenstern \citep[\S28]{vnm1953} call a zero-sum cooperative
game fair when its coalition value depends only on coalition size. This is
neither equality at every normal-form profile nor an assignment preference.
The first-edition text has not been inspected. The migrated research notes
retain detailed source versions and access limitations; historical priority
and the final Plan article require further source work.
