\section{Strategic properties preserved}
Cardinal maps transport independent mixed profiles by marginal pushforward and
satisfy $U_{p(i)}(gx)=a_iU_i(x)+b_i$. They therefore transport mixed best
responses, mixed Nash equilibria and correlated-equilibrium incentive inequalities.
Ordinal maps preserve pure best responses against pure opponents and pure Nash
equilibria, but need not preserve mixed equilibria.

For an exact counterexample, let the two payoff matrices be
\[
u_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&3\\2&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
u_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&4\\9&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The bare player swap is ordinal since $u_1(Ps)=u_0(s)^2$, and conversely by
square root on the attained nonnegative levels. If $p,q$ denote probabilities
of action 0, row indifference gives $q=1/2$ and column indifference gives
$p=2/3$. Thus $(2/3,1/2)$ is a mixed equilibrium, while its swapped profile
$(1/2,2/3)$ is not. The two pure equilibria are exchanged correctly.

The binary exact-potential theorem belongs to the companion symmetry project.
Its cardinal and ordinal normalization consequences are preserved in the
research ledger as dependencies, not claimed here as independent discoveries.
