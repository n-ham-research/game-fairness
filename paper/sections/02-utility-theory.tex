\section{Utility theory and typed transports}
Let $I=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$, let each $A_i$ be finite and nonempty, and put
$A=\prod_i A_i$. A game has payoffs $u_i:A\to\mathbb R$.
A typed bijection $g=(p;(f_i))$ has a permutation $p$ of players and bijections
$f_i:A_i\to A_{p(i)}$. Its action is $(gs)_{p(i)}=f_i(s_i)$.
Composition is right to left, so $(P_p s)_j=s_{p^{-1}(j)}$ for bare permutations.
Write $q(g)=p$.

\begin{definition}
A typed bijection is exact if $u_{p(i)}(gs)=u_i(s)$; cardinal if
$u_{p(i)}(gs)=a_i u_i(s)+b_i$ with $a_i>0$; and ordinal if, for all $s,t$,
\[
u_i(s)\le u_i(t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u_{p(i)}(gs)\le u_{p(i)}(gt).
\]
The latter preserves the complete pure-outcome preorder, including ties.
\end{definition}
Ordinality is equivalent to a strictly increasing bijection between attained
payoff ranges. Cardinality corresponds to preservation of expected-utility
preferences over all lotteries on profiles. To see necessity, pull back a
target representation $v$. For nonconstant $u$, choose worst and best profiles
$a,b$. Each profile $s$ is indifferent to the lottery on $a,b$ with probability
$(u(s)-u(a))/(u(b)-u(a))$ on $b$. Preserved indifference forces $v=\alpha u+\beta$,
and strict endpoint preference gives $\alpha>0$. Constant tables are immediate.
Sufficiency follows by linearity. These are representation statements, not
interpersonal utility comparisons. Ham \citep[\S4.3]{hamthesis} already studies
the finite-game counterparts; the migrated proof also treats all independent
mixed-profile comparisons without assuming product laws form a convex set.
